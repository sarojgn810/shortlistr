Evaluate the job posting against the candidate CV.

Return ONLY valid JSON (no markdown fences) with this structure:
{
  "must_haves": [{"req": "the requirement, quoted from the JD",
                  "met": true|false,
                  "evidence": "the CV line that satisfies it, or \"\" if unmet"}],
  "score": 0.0 to 5.0,

First list must_haves: the posting's HARD requirements only — the things a
recruiter would screen on. Not nice-to-haves, not culture. Mark "met" only when
the CV shows direct evidence, and quote that evidence. An unmet requirement is
normal and useful; do not mark something met to be generous.

Then score 0.0-5.0 on how well THIS CV matches THIS posting:

  5.0        Every must-have met with direct evidence, and seniority, domain
             and location all line up. Rare.
  4.0-4.9    Strong. All must-haves met; the gaps are nice-to-haves.
  3.0-3.9    Plausible. Most must-haves met, one real gap needing a stretch.
  2.0-2.9    Weak. Several must-haves unmet, or seniority or domain is off.
  1.0-1.9    Poor. Wrong track, or almost nothing carries over.
  0.0-0.9    Not a real or valid opening — expired, a scam, or an ad.

The score must follow the must_haves list you just wrote. If two of six are
unmet, that is not a 4. Judge this posting on its own evidence rather than
against how good the candidate is in general — a strong CV does not make every
posting a strong match.

Cite specific JD requirements in blocks.

Writing style for block strings:
- Concrete and specific — name tools, requirements, and numbers from the JD/CV.
- Active voice. No banned fluff (leverage, utilize, robust, cutting-edge, passionate, delve, synergy…).
- No throat-clearing, binary contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y."), or summary-recap endings.
- Do not invent employers, metrics, or skills absent from the CV/JD.
